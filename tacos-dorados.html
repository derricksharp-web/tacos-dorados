Tacos Dorados with Chorizo and Potato

Ingredients

  • 1 lb. chorizo
  • 4–5 medium potatoes per pound of chorizo
  • Corn tortillas
  • Neutral, high-smoke-point oil for frying
  • Shredded cabbage (optional)
  • Sour cream or Mexican crema (optional)
  • Salsa (optional)
  • Lime (optional)

Scaling the recipe: Exact measurements are not necessary. A good starting ratio is 4–5 medium potatoes for every pound of chorizo. Increase or decrease the ingredients based on how many tacos you want to prepare.

Directions

  1. Prepare the Potatoes

    Peel the potatoes and boil them until they are soft and easily mashed. Drain the potatoes and allow them to cool slightly.

  2. Cook the Chorizo

    Cook the chorizo in a frying pan over medium-low heat until fully cooked. Medium-low heat helps prevent the chorizo from burning.

    Do not discard the rendered fat from the chorizo. It will be added to the potatoes and will help flavor the filling.

  3. Prepare the Filling

    Mash the cooked potatoes, leaving them slightly chunky. Avoid mashing them completely smooth because an overly soft filling is more likely to leak out of the tortillas during frying.

    Add the cooked chorizo and rendered fat to the potatoes. Gently fold everything together until the chorizo is evenly distributed throughout the potatoes.

    Taste the filling and add salt only if needed. Because chorizo is already seasoned and can be salty, additional salt may not be necessary.

  4. Warm the Tortillas

    Warm the corn tortillas one at a time in a dry pan until they become soft and flexible. The goal is to make the tortillas pliable enough to fold without cracking.

    Place the warmed tortillas in a tortilla keeper or wrap them in a clean kitchen towel so they remain warm while you prepare the tacos.

  5. Fill the Tacos

    Place approximately 2 tablespoons of the chorizo and potato filling on one half of each tortilla, then fold the tortilla in half.

    Avoid overfilling the tacos. Too much filling can fall out during frying and make the tacos more difficult to turn.

  6. Cook Now or Prepare Ahead

    The tacos can be fried immediately, but freezing them before frying is recommended. Freezing helps the tacos hold their shape and makes them easier to handle during frying.

    To freeze the tacos, find a plate that will fit inside a gallon-size freezer bag. Arrange the tacos on the plate, place the plate inside the bag, seal it, and freeze. Repeat as needed until all tacos are stored.

    The tacos do not need to be thawed before frying.

  7. Fry the Tacos

    Add enough neutral cooking oil to a frying pan to completely cover the bottom. The tacos should be pan-fried rather than submerged in oil.

    Preheat the oil before adding the tacos.

    • From frozen: Use medium heat so the tacos become hot in the center without over-browning the tortillas.
    • Fresh or thawed: Use medium-high heat.

    Add the tacos to the hot oil without overcrowding the pan. Do not stack the tacos. Each taco should have good contact with the pan and oil so the tortilla browns and crisps evenly.

    Cook until the first side is golden and crispy. Flip each taco and cook the second side until golden and crisp. Remove the finished tacos to a serving plate.

    Check the oil level between batches and add additional oil as needed.

  8. Serve

    Serve the tacos plain or allow everyone to add their preferred toppings, including shredded cabbage, sour cream or Mexican crema, salsa, and fresh lime.
